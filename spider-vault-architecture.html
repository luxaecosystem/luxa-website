Spider Vault Architecture & Mainnet Migration

Client-side custody, cryptographic key protection and the path from mined Shards to native LUXA · 9 min read

The Spider Vault and the mainnet migration framework are two of the foundational components of the LUXA Ecosystem. The first protects the cryptographic keys that give you control of your funds. The second defines how activity accumulated inside the Telegram Mini App is verified and settled on the native luxa-1 blockchain.

Together, they are built around three principles: client-side security, controlled identity verification, and resistance to large-scale Sybil activity.

1. The Spider Vault Architecture

The Spider Vault is a decentralized, non-custodial custody matrix integrated directly into the LUXA Telegram Mini App. It is designed around a zero-knowledge-oriented model: private secrets stay on the user's device rather than being sent to backend infrastructure or stored as plaintext database records.

Client-Side Key Isolation

Key generation, encryption, decryption and transaction signing all happen locally, inside the sandbox of the user's device — browser or mobile webview. This separates the blockchain interface from the sensitive cryptographic material that controls an account: the backend never needs access to your plaintext private key.

Multi-Vault Mesh

Each Telegram account can initialize and manage up to 10 independent vaults, each with its own native address in Bech32 format using the luxa1... prefix. Different blockchain identities can be kept independent from one another while remaining accessible from the same Mini App.

2. Private-Key Protection

PBKDF2 Key Derivation

Encrypted private-key material is protected with PBKDF2 (Password-Based Key Derivation Function 2). Your locally chosen numerical PIN is combined with a cryptographically generated salt and run through a large number of HMAC-SHA256 iterations, making repeated PIN guesses computationally expensive compared to a simple hash comparison. The overall security depends on the iteration count, salt generation, PIN entropy and implementation.

AES-256-GCM Authenticated Encryption

The resulting encrypted key payload is protected with AES-256-GCM (Galois/Counter Mode), which provides both confidentiality and integrity. Alongside encryption, GCM produces an authentication tag — if the ciphertext is altered, tag verification fails and the payload is not decrypted.

Security principle: encryption alone isn't enough for secure key storage. Authenticated encryption such as AES-GCM also detects unauthorized modification of protected ciphertext.

3. From Mined Shards to Native LUXA

The migration framework bridges activity accumulated inside the Mini App with settlement on the luxa-1 Layer 1. Conceptually:

Mining activity → Shard accumulation
↓
Eligibility verification
↓
Identity / Sybil verification
↓
Migration settlement
↓
Native LUXA on luxa-1

The protocol description defines the eligibility and settlement conditions but does not itself fix a numerical Shard-to-LUXA conversion ratio — the exact amount of native LUXA generated from a given Shard balance can't be calculated from these parameters alone.

4. The Three Mainnet Migration Requirements

Requirement #1 — 60 Active Mining Days

A participant must accumulate a minimum of 60 active 24-hour mining sessions. This establishes a temporal participation threshold, distinguishing sustained participation from accounts created solely for opportunistic reward extraction.

Requirement #2 — Biometric Identity Verification

Migration to mainnet requires identity verification through Sumsub, including 3D liveness and document checks. The goal is to establish a one-person-to-one protocol identity relationship, making large-scale Sybil structures substantially harder to run.

Requirement #3 — 8% Settlement Fee

At final settlement and bridging, an 8% protocol fee applies, directed to the Community Treasury Reserve:

5. Understanding the Settlement Calculation

Even without an official Shard-to-LUXA rate, the settlement process can be expressed mathematically. Let S be eligible Shards, R the protocol-defined conversion rate, G gross LUXA before fees, and N net LUXA after settlement:

G = S × R
F = G × 0.08
N = G × 0.92 = S × R × 0.92

The key unknown is R. Until it is published, this should be read as the settlement model, not as a fixed economic conversion rate.

A Practical Example

Take a hypothetical participant with 100,000 eligible Shards who has met all migration requirements. If the protocol were to define a hypothetical rate of 1 LUXA per 100 Shards:

  • Gross: 100,000 ÷ 100 = 1,000 LUXA
  • Fee (8%): 1,000 × 0.08 = 80 LUXA
  • Net settlement: 1,000 − 80 = 920 LUXA

Illustrative only — the 1:100 ratio is not an official LUXA protocol parameter.

6. Genesis Exemption — Citizen Zero

The protocol defines a special genesis identity, Citizen Zero (#0), with architectural root-genesis immunity, exempt from the standard migration constraints and settlement fee — a protocol-level exception rather than a standard participant pathway.

7. Why the Two-Layer Security Model Matters

LUXA separates two problems that are often treated as one:

  • Cryptographic security — the Spider Vault, protecting private-key material client-side with PBKDF2 and AES-256-GCM.
  • Identity & Sybil resistance — the migration framework, controlling eligibility for mainnet settlement.

Conclusion

A participant first accumulates eligible Shards through sustained mining. The account then satisfies the 60-day activity threshold, completes Sumsub identity verification, and passes the protocol's migration conditions. At settlement, the eligible balance is converted at the protocol-defined Shard-to-LUXA rate, with an 8% fee allocated to the Community Treasury Reserve — while the Spider Vault keeps key material on-device throughout, protected by PBKDF2 and AES-256-GCM.
